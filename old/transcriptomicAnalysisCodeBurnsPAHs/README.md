# Superfund Research Program Analytics

<img src="../OSU-PNNLsuperfund_Small.png"  width="300">


This folder contains code that was used in analyzing and visualizing transcriptomic data presented in "Differential toxicity of wildfire-derived PAH mixtures in a human respiratory epithelial-macrophage co-culture model" (Burns et al., 2026). Fastq and normalized count data files can be found on NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus using accession GSE329482.

## Paper Abstract:
Wildfires are a growing public health concern with wildfire occurrence, duration and intensity increasing in recent years. However, significant data gaps exist in understanding toxicity from inhalation of wildfire smoke emissions, which are chemically complex containing polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), among other pollutants. In the respiratory system, induction of airway inflammation is orchestrated through signaling between airway epithelial cells and macrophages, which are two of the most abundant cell types in the respiratory tract contributing to the innate immune response. Activation of airway inflammation is a critical event leading to adverse health effects and damage to the respiratory system after chemical exposure. To evaluate the combined contribution of airway epithelial-macrophage responses to PAHs from wildfires, we utilized a co-culture model consisting of organotypic primary human bronchial epithelial cells (HBEC) differentiated at the air-liquid interface and THP-1 macrophages and evaluated the response to two wildfire PAH mixtures, SuperWildfire12 (SW12) and SuperFireMAX (SFM). These mixtures represent the most abundant PAHs from passive sampling during wildfires, with SFM including PAHs with only four or more aromatic rings. Barrier integrity, cytotoxicity, cytokine expression (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF), PAH biomarkers for chemical metabolism (CYP1A1, CYP1B1), and global gene expression were evaluated after 24 h of exposure. Additionally, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry to evaluate Annexin V staining as well as epithelial subtype responses to mixture treatment. The higher molecular weight PAHs in SFM resulted in decreased barrier integrity (p < 0.05) and altered transcriptional responses through the aryl hydrocarbon receptor. In contrast, the predominately lower molecular weight PAHs in SW12 mediated an inflammatory response through increased gene expression of IL6, CXCL8 and TNF cytokines and transcriptional signaling via NF-κB that was only apparent in the co-culture model, but not when HBEC were cultured alone. In addition, cell type-specific responses were observed in the co-culture for cytotoxicity and CYP1A1 expression. These findings highlight unique mechanisms associated with two representative mixtures of PAHs in wildfire smoke and provide evidence for the importance of co-culture models in understanding toxicity. 

## Experiment details:
Fully differentiated bronchial epithelial cells in the presence or absence of THP-1 macrophages (n=4 replicates) were treated with 300 µM SuperWildfire12, 300 µM SuperFireMAX, or vehicle control (DPBS with 1% DMSO) for 24 hrs. Isolated mRNA were sequenced on a BGISEQ-500 platform. Primary HBEC (Lonza) were expanded and transferred to transwell inserts for differentiation at the air-liquid interface in 24-well plates each containing 500 uL of assay medium utilizing the PneumaCult™ ALI medium kit (STEMCELL Technologies, Vancouver, CA). Cells were cultured at 37C, 5% CO2 with media changes every 48 to 72 hours. On day 30, transwell inserts with fully differentiating HBEC were inverted and THP-1 macrophages were seeded onto the apical side of the membrane. After 2 hours, the insert was returned to cell culture plate. HBEC were washed with DPBS and then exposed on the apical surface for 24 hours to either 25 uL of 300 µM SuperWildfire12 (SW12), 300 µM SuperFireMAX (SFM), or vehicle (1% DMSO in DPBS). HBEC were scraped and collected from inserts. Total RNA was isolated from HBEC (n=4) using RNeasy Mini Kits (Qiagen) and was quantified on a Synergy HTX plate reader equipped with a Take3 module (BioTek). RNA quality was evaluated based on 260/280 ratio and by examining 18S and 28S peaks. Acceptable RNA quality was based on RIN/RQN ≥ 7. mRNA was poly-A selected, and libraries were prepared as previously described (Dasgupta et al., 2022). Reads were annotated and mapped to the human genome assembly GRCh38.p14 using STAR. Differential expression was determined using DESeq2 compared to vehicle control where p-adj<=0.05.
